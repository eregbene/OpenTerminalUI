# Forensic research report: why fib618 and Bennett look good over 3 months (2026-11-03)

**Diagnostic only.** Nothing changed in live, paper, strategy parameters or filters. P3 was not run. Every number here comes from files that already existed:
- P2 v2 trades: `reports/experiments/p2v2_2026-11-03/trades_v2_live_rules.csv`, Aug 3 – Nov 3 2026, observed costs, live Friday rules.
- Time Machine trades: `logs/tm_check_2026-07-26_fx.csv`, 155 trades.

The one new run is a **count of where Bensim setups are rejected**, with no P&L (`bensim/bennett_funnel.py`, registered).

P2 regime and session splits are used here **only to explain**, never as filters. Every proposal below is a hypothesis to test on data not used before (2021-10 → 2026-06 MT5 M5, or FXCM H1 2012-2026, as stated).

## Headline
fib618's 3-month result rests on a few large winners, and the biggest one is a **single news-driven trade that two strategies took together**:
- **Top 5 winners:** they make 108 % of fib618's +26.5R. Without the top 3 the total is +4.7R; without the top 5 it's −2.2R.
- **The largest winner:** a GBPUSD short entered Sep 9 11:30 UTC, with a planned reward:risk of **13.6**. It hit target on Sep 10 12:35, minutes after the **ECB rate decision (12:15) and US PPI (12:30)**.
- **Enigma took the same trade** (same symbol, side and entry bar) for +9.99R.

That one event explains most of fib618's September, most of its "London" result, and most of the 0.66 fib618/enigma correlation. Bennett's low trade count is built into its rules: it needs about 9 conditions to line up, and at ~20 trades a year on 6 symbols it **can't be validated in any realistic time**.

## 1. fib618 forensic review (entry, exit, stop, target, signal)
Code: `strategy.plan` with `zone_mode="fib"`, live params `weekly_check.LIVE`.

| Part | What the code does | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Signal | M15 close beyond the 20-bar Donchian channel by ≥ 0.3 ATR14, ATR14/SMA50(ATR) ≥ 1.1, H1 swing trend agrees (strategy.py:234) | a breakout continuation idea |
| Leg | origin = lowest low (longs) of the 20-bar window; leg end = highest high up to the **signal bar only** (237-239) | the fib is **frozen at the breakout bar**. If price extends before pulling back, "0.618" is a much shallower retracement of the real move. Matches the 5y loss study (78 % of losers extended before the fill, then reversed fully). |
| Entry | first M5 touch of 0.618 within 48 M15 bars (12 h), **market at that M5 close** (order_for) | the actual entry can be deeper or shallower than 0.618; R is measured from the real entry |
| Stop | leg origin ∓ 0.1 ATR (the 100 % level) | wide stop; R:R depends on leg size |
| Target | **open of the bar after the signal ± 4 ATR(signal)** (269), not tied to the leg; rejected if R:R > 20 (`max_rr`) | R:R ranges 2.1 – 13.6 (median 2.9). The result depends on rare far targets. |
| Confirmations | 2 of (killzone, POI, sweep) | **killzone = 07-10 / 12-15 UTC**, so 85 of 92 trades are in those hours by construction. The session result is partly built in. |
| Exit | stop / target / 200 M15 bars (50 h) / Friday 20:00 UTC (live) | holds overnight and through scheduled news (no news block live) |

**Exits in P2:** 53 stops (−54.2R), 22 targets (+72.6R), 14 Friday closes (+5.9R), 3 time exits (+2.2R).

**By planned R:R:**
- 2–4: 79 trades, +0.14R each.
- 4–8: 6 trades, −0.01R.
- above 8: 1 trade, **+12.99R**.

No logic bug was found. The fragility comes from the design: a breakout-continuation idea with a far, ATR-based target, held through news.

## 2. September concentration and the London session
- **September:** 33 trades, +21.3R of the +26.5R.
  - Week 37 (Sep 7-13) alone: +13.0R on GBPUSD, the ECB/PPI trade.
  - EURUSD and GBPUSD **shorts** (long USD) in September: 10 trades, +26.4R. The other 23 September trades: −5.1R.
  - So the month is one USD move plus one central-bank event.
- **London 07-12 UTC:** 17 trades, +22.4R, but the +12.99R trade is one of them. Without it: 16 trades, +9.4R (+0.59R each). That's small and not separable from killzone selection.
- **Explanation, not a filter:** the 3-month positive is what you'd expect from a far-target breakout system in a month with a strong, news-driven USD move. It isn't evidence of a repeatable session effect.

## 3. Bennett: why so few trades
The funnel counts every M15 boundary from Aug 3 to Nov 3 on its 5 live pairs plus XAUUSD: 37,713 calls in total. Rejections in order, from `reports/experiments/bennett_funnel_2026-11-03/funnel.csv`:

| Rule that ended the call | Calls |
|---|---|
| outside killzones 07-11 / 13-17 UTC | 24,181 (64 %) |
| Sunday bar | 859 |
| H4 structure doesn't agree with the H1 leg | 4,122 |
| relief hasn't reached the OTE (62-79 %) | 3,656 |
| no M5 CHoCH **on this exact bar** | 3,208 |
| setup older than 48 h | 1,231 |
| close beyond the protected level (invalid) | 432 |
| 30-minute structure not turned our way (`agree_tf`) | 12 |
| no target pays ≥ 2.75R | 9 |
| obstructing imbalance (`clear_path`) | 1 |
| **TRIGGERED** | **1** (the backtest also checks on M5 touches: 5 trades) |

Only **23 calls** get past the CHoCH trigger, and the last three rules remove almost all of them. Frequency: ~5 trades per 3 months, about 20 a year. The 433 trades P3-style validation needs would take **> 20 years** on these symbols. The 5-year setup database (no live filters) was negative after costs.

## 4. The 0.66 fib618/enigma correlation
- Daily net-R correlation is 0.66 over the period. **Drop the 3 largest co-movement days and it's 0.17.**
- 17 days had P&L from both. 6 of enigma's 24 trades overlap a fib618 trade on the same symbol and side. Those 6 make **+12.7R; the other 18 make −4.2R.**
- **Cause: the same trades, not a shared market factor.** Both strategies fire on the same impulse/pullback, and on Sep 9 they entered the same bar. The portfolio therefore holds fewer independent bets than its trade count suggests.

## 5. Market regime
- **Prior evidence is weak.** The fib618 5-year regime study (breakout follow-through, variance ratio, D1 efficiency, D1 ATR percentile, own last-20 R) found every feature non-monotonic, |t| < 2.2.
- **The P2 splits are small and post hoc.** Enigma range +0.92R (13 trades) vs trend −0.32R (11 trades); fib618 by session. These must not become filters.
- **"Regime" may be a news calendar effect.** The September result is one event. A causal event/regime label (scheduled high-impact news within the holding window) is a better candidate than trend/range labels.

## 6. One-trade-per-pair rule and shared-account exposure
- **The rule blocked 36 of 201 live-set trades (−1.1R in total).**
  - 32 were **same-direction duplicates** (+3.0R).
  - 4 were opposite-side trades (−4.1R).
  - By strategy: imbalance 20 trades (−11.4R), fib618 12 (+3.3R), enigma 4 (+7.0R).
- **It mostly removes duplicate bets.** It doesn't pick out losers. Net R per trade rose from +0.211 to +0.264 because the drawdown and the variance fell (max DD 25.7R → 17.6R).
- **Live-sized worst case:**
  - Up to 4 positions open at once.
  - Up to **1.75 % of equity** at risk, assuming fib618 at its full 0.5 % with books and Bennett at 0.25 %.
  - Up to **4R of net USD exposure in one direction.**
  - Worst day −4.9R; worst week −12.5R.
- **The paper runner's sizing bug** (each order checked against a fresh account) is separate. The fix is tested, not deployed.

## 7. Can the Time Machine tell good conditions from bad ones before entry?
Tested on the 155 tm_check trades (Jul 26 – Oct 26), the only trades with TM values.

| Value known at entry | AUC (win) | Correlation with R, after accounting for log planned R:R |
|---|---|---|
| bensim TM target-first | 0.559 | **+0.078 (t 0.96)** |
| bensim TM stop within 1 h | 0.414 | +0.027 (t 0.33) |
| repo TM (60-day drift kept) avg R | 0.410 | −0.119 (t −1.48) |
| **planned R:R alone, 1/(1+RR)** | **0.586** | — |

- **The bensim TM's odds are mostly the trade's geometry.** They correlate 0.55 with 1/(1+RR), and once R:R is accounted for almost nothing is left. That's what the null model is designed to do.
- **Calibration:** in every quartile, real target hits exceed the TM's predicted rate by 7–15 points. That gap is this sample's apparent edge (in-sample) plus a horizon mismatch: the TM runs to Friday, the trades had other exits. So the gap isn't a signal of its own.
- **Answer: no, not as it stands.** Its useful job is as a **null baseline**. Edge should show up as real target-hit rate minus the TM's noise rate on unseen data.

## Toward the bigger objective (finding conditions with a repeatable edge)
The audit points to three building blocks that already exist and aren't wired together yet:
1. **A null model per trade:** the bensim TM (direction removed) gives the noise rate for any stop/target geometry.
2. **Causal condition labels known at entry:** news calendar, session, D1 state, all available before entry.
3. **The validation engine and registry:** DSR with an honest trial count, block bootstrap, effective sample size, PBO.

**The workflow:**
- Define conditions **before** looking at outcomes.
- Measure each strategy's excess over the null model per condition, on data never used for selection.
- Accept a condition→strategy pair only if it passes DSR and Holm over the conditions tested.
- "No demonstrated advantage" is the default state, so not trading is the default.

R1, R2 and R6 are the first instances of this.

## Limitations
- Bennett's funnel was checked at M15 boundaries. The backtest also checks on M5 zone touches, so the absolute counts are a lower bound; the order of the rejections is what matters.
- The TM analysis uses the Oct 29 set (155 trades, Jul 26 – Oct 26). It overlaps the P2 period and is in-sample.
- The live-risk figure assumes fib618 always at its full 0.5 %. Weak setups use half.
